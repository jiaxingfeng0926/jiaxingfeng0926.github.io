## I. Introduction

Software engineering today is no longer just about writing code from scratch; it also involves learning how to work effectively with tools that can accelerate or reshape the development process. Throughout this course, I treated AI as one of those tools rather than as a shortcut to answers. Instead of relying on it to replace problem-solving, I used AI to clarify unclear concepts, identify mistakes I could not immediately see, and improve the quality of both my code and written work. Tools such as ChatGPT and AI-assisted coding features similar to GitHub Copilot became part of my workflow in a limited and intentional way, allowing me to stay engaged with the material while still benefiting from faster feedback and alternative perspectives.

---
## II. Personal Experience with AI in ICS 314

Below, I reflect on how I used—or chose not to use—AI for each major course element.

### Experience WODs

For Experience WODs, especially those involving functional programming or unfamiliar libraries, I would use AI as a starting point. This helped me understand how to begin when tutorials were outdated or unclear. However, I often needed to revise the output significantly to meet the exact requirements, which reinforced learning rather than replacing it.

### In-class Practice WODs

I used AI minimally during in-class practice WODs. Since these exercises were meant to reinforce immediate understanding, I preferred attempting them on my own first. When I did consult AI, it was mainly to verify logic or confirm syntax rather than generate solutions.

### In-class WODs

For in-class WODs, I used AI frequently due to strict time constraints. AI provided immediate guidance on what to focus on next and helped resolve blocking issues quickly. I used it as a problem-solving aid rather than copying full solutions, focusing on understanding the reasoning so I could complete the WOD efficiently.

### Essays

AI was very useful for essays, particularly for grammar checking, improving flow, and enhancing structure. For example, I would write a draft myself and then ask ChatGPT to *“check grammar and improve clarity while keeping my original tone.”* This helped me catch mistakes and made the essay more polished without sacrificing authenticity.

### Final Project

During the final project, AI was used heavily for debugging, brainstorming, and system understanding rather than blindly writing code. I used AI to troubleshoot backend issues, resolve merge conflicts, understand Prisma and PostgreSQL behavior, and clarify Next.js patterns. It was especially helpful when dealing with Vercel database login errors and backend configuration problems.

### Learning a Concept or Tutorial

When learning new concepts such as Prisma schemas, ERDs, or API routing, AI helped explain ideas in a way that was easier for me to understand than some documentation. I often asked AI to rephrase explanations or walk through examples step by step.

### Answering a Question in Class or Discord

AI helped me prepare clearer explanations before asking or answering questions in Discord. It allowed me to form better “smart questions” by organizing my thoughts and identifying exactly where I was confused.

### Asking or Answering a Smart Question

Instead of asking vague questions, I used AI to refine my questions. For example, I would describe my issue and ask AI what additional details were needed to make the question clearer, which improved communication with teammates and instructors.

### Coding Examples

For coding examples, AI-generated snippets helped illustrate how data transformations work in practice. I used these examples to analyze control flow and behavior before writing my own implementation.

### Explaining Code

AI was particularly useful for explaining existing code. If I encountered code I didn’t fully understand, I would ask AI to explain it line by line.

### Writing Code

I used AI primarily to help outline the structure of a solution before writing code myself. After implementing my own version, I used AI to review and suggest improvements, which allowed me to refine my code while maintaining control and understanding of the final implementation.

### Documenting Code

AI was helpful for writing documentation templates and improving clarity. I still customized documentation to match the project’s structure and terminology.

### Quality Assurance

AI was extremely helpful for QA tasks such as fixing ESLint errors or identifying what was wrong with a specific piece of code. Asking questions like *“What’s wrong with this code?”* saved time and reduced frustration.

### Other Uses

I did not use AI beyond the cases described above.

## III. Impact on Learning and Understanding

Overall, AI influenced how I approached learning rather than simply making tasks easier. It was most effective when used to uncover gaps in my understanding or confirm reasoning I was unsure about. At the same time, I became aware that relying on it too quickly could short-circuit the learning process, which pushed me to use AI as a support tool instead of a default solution.

---

## IV. Practical Applications Outside ICS 314

Beyond ICS 314, I have seen how AI can be applied to real-world software engineering tasks such as system design, debugging pipelines, and backend optimization. These applications reflect realistic industry use cases where AI acts as a productivity tool rather than an autonomous developer.

---

## V. Challenges and Opportunities

One major challenge of using AI is that it can provide incorrect or overly complex solutions. It can also disrupt an existing codebase by introducing unfamiliar patterns. These risks highlighted the importance of specificity in prompting and verifying results. At the same time, there is an opportunity to integrate AI more formally into software engineering education by teaching prompt engineering and responsible usage.

---

## VI. Comparative Analysis

Compared to traditional teaching methods, AI-enhanced learning offers faster feedback and increased accessibility. However, traditional methods still play a crucial role in developing discipline and foundational understanding. The best learning experience comes from combining both approaches.

---

## VII. Future Considerations

In the future, AI will likely become even more integrated into software engineering education. The key will be teaching students how to use AI thoughtfully—leveraging it for learning, debugging, and productivity without allowing it to replace critical thinking.

---

## VIII. Conclusion

My experience with AI in this course was both practical and eye-opening. I relied on AI frequently, especially when time was limited or when I felt stuck, and it became a consistent part of my workflow rather than an occasional aid. At the same time, this reliance forced me to be more intentional about verifying results and understanding what I was using. The course ultimately showed me that using AI effectively in software engineering is less about avoiding it and more about knowing when to trust it, when to question it, and how to learn from it without losing ownership of the work.
